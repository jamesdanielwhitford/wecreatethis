# Gochujang chicken ramen for four, shopped entirely at Woolworths

Serves 4. Prep 20 minutes, cook 40 minutes. T is a tablespoon (15 ml), t is a teaspoon (5 ml).

## Ingredients

### Broth

- 1.5 L Fresh Free Range Liquid Chicken Stock (3 x 500 ml)
- 4 T Hikari Karu-Mu Koji Miso Paste
- 3 T gochujang paste
- 3 T light soy sauce
- 1.5 T sesame oil
- 4 cloves garlic, finely grated
- 20 g fresh ginger, finely grated
- 1 t sugar

No water. Plain stock is thinner and less salty than a ramen broth concentrate, so the miso goes to 4 T and the soy sauce goes into the broth.

### Chicken

- 4 boneless chicken breasts, about 600 to 700 g
- 4 T light soy sauce
- 2 t sugar
- 1 t salt

### Noodles and toppings

- 2 x 200 g packs Ramen Noodles
- 350 g mushrooms, sliced 5 mm thick
- 1.5 T butter
- 1.5 T dark soy sauce
- 400 g pak choi, 100 g per person (2 x 200 g Baby Pak Choi, or 1 x 350 g Pak Choi)
- 4 eggs
- 100 g spring onions, finely sliced
- White sesame seeds, toasted

Baby pak choi: trim the root end, halve each head lengthways, quarter anything bigger than your palm. Large pak choi: separate the leaves, cut the white stems into 3 cm pieces, keep the green tops whole and separate.

## Method

1. **Marinate the chicken.** Mix 2 T light soy sauce with the sugar and salt. Add the breasts and turn to coat. Leave 20 minutes at room temperature, or overnight in the fridge. Reserve the other 2 T soy sauce.
2. **Soft-boil the eggs.** Six and a half minutes in boiling water, then straight into iced water. Peel once cold, halve just before serving.
3. **Bloom the aromatics.** In your largest pot, warm the sesame oil over medium heat. Add the garlic and ginger, fry 60 to 90 seconds until fragrant but not browned. Add the gochujang and sugar and fry another two minutes, stirring, until it darkens slightly and smells sweet rather than sharp.
4. **Add the stock.** Pour in all 1.5 L of stock and the 3 T light soy sauce. Bring to a bare simmer and hold there for 20 minutes.
5. **Poach the chicken.** Slip the breasts into the broth and hold it at a bare quiver, no bubbles breaking the surface, for 12 to 16 minutes, until 74C in the thickest part. Lift out, rest 5 minutes, then cut across the grain into 1 cm slices. To sear instead: pat dry, medium-high heat in a little oil, 5 to 6 minutes the first side, 3 to 5 the second, dropping to medium after the flip if thicker than 2.5 cm.
6. **Whisk in the miso.** Ladle a cup of hot broth into a bowl, whisk the 4 T miso into it until smooth, then stir back into the pot. Do not boil it after this. Taste and adjust: more miso or soy for salt, more gochujang for heat.
7. **The mushrooms.** Dry-sear in a wide pan in batches, no oil and no lid, until they have given up their water and browned at the edges. Add the butter, cook a minute, then the dark soy sauce until the liquid has gone and they are glazed. Tip into a bowl.
8. **The pak choi.** Same pan over medium-high heat with a splash of water. Baby heads cut-side down, or the white stems first if using a large head. Cover, steam 2 minutes. Add the leafy tops, cover 1 minute more. Stems tender with a little bite, leaves just collapsed. Pinch of salt, remove.
9. **The tare.** Wipe the pan, heat to high, add the reserved 2 T light soy sauce, reduce one to two minutes to a thick caramel.
10. **Noodles, last.** Boil a large pot of water, cook the noodles 3 to 4 minutes, drain, serve immediately. Do not cook them in the broth.
11. **Assemble.** Soy caramel into four warmed bowls, then noodles, then hot broth. Chicken, mushrooms and half an egg on top, two pak choi halves against the side. Spring onions, sesame seeds, a few drops of sesame oil.

Steps 1 and 2 can happen hours ahead. Cook the pak choi and noodles once everyone is sitting down.

## Adjustments

- **Too spicy.** A teaspoon of honey or sugar into the broth.
- **Creamy.** A splash of coconut milk or 2 T cream at the end, off the heat.
- **More depth.** Char an onion half and a few spring onion roots in a dry pan until blackened, simmer in the broth, fish out before serving.
- **Nutty body.** A tablespoon of tahini or smooth peanut butter whisked in with the miso.
