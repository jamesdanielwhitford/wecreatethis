# Session 012 - 2026-07-29

Reverse-engineering Merlin's "Likely Birds" ordering so Bird Bingo cards reflect the birds
a player is genuinely most likely to see. Research only - **no changes to app code.**

## Summary

James could not reproduce, from the eBird API, the ordered species list Merlin shows for
his area. He supplied that list by hand (24 species, in order) as ground truth and asked to
work out which API calls reproduce it.

Two findings, one of them a real bug:

1. **Merlin's "Likely Birds" is eBird bar-chart frequency for the region, sliced to the
   current week of the year.** Confirmed at 88% recall / Kendall tau +0.667 against City of
   Tshwane (`ZA-GT-08`). The data lives behind Cornell SSO, not the public API.
2. **Bird Bingo's current ranking cannot work, for a reason unrelated to radius tuning.**
   `/data/obs/geo/recent` returns one *de-duplicated* row per species, so the occurrence
   counter in `bingo.js` `fetchCandidates()` always lands on 1. The `"N checklists"` metric
   is fictional and the resulting order is arbitrary.

All work lives in `birdle-tests/merlin-reverse-engineering/` (untracked).

## The bug (root cause of the mismatch)

`bingo.js:41-58` ranks candidates by counting occurrences per `speciesCode` from
`/data/obs/geo/recent`. Measured at 25km/back=30: **183 records, 183 distinct species,
maximum count of 1 for every species.**

The endpoint already de-duplicates to the most recent record per species. So the counter is
incrementing over a list where nothing repeats, every species ties at 1, and the order out
is eBird's arbitrary return order. Radius tuning changes *which* birds appear but never
their ranking - which is exactly why previous tuning attempts never helped.

Visible in the comparison page: the current method leads with Rameron Pigeon,
Sulphur-breasted Bushshrike and Rosy-faced Lovebird (an escaped cage bird) for a Faerie Glen
garden.

## Method comparison (clean run)

**Top-9** = how many of a method's top 9 are anywhere on Merlin's list. For a 3x3 card this
matters more than recall@24, since the card only draws from the top.

| Method | Recall@24 | Top-9 | Tau | Time | Calls |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bar chart (SSO)** | **21/24** | - | **+0.67** | ~1s | **1** |
| **Hotspot nearest** (James's pick #1) | 17/24 | **9/9** | +0.50 | 10s | 212 |
| **Nearest, distance-weighted** (pick #2) | 16/24 | 7/9 | **+0.52** | 11s | 212 |
| Hotspot richest | 14-17/24 | 8/9 | +0.25..+0.49 | 11-19s | 212 |
| Region checklists (pick #3) | 18/24 | 8/9 | +0.29 | 9s | 202 |
| Current app, 25km | 9/24 | 3/9 | +0.17 | 0.5s | 1 |
| Current app, 8km | 8/24 | 4/9 | -0.14 | 0.6s | 1 |

## Decisions

- **James's judgement of the lists beats the aggregate scores, and was demonstrably ahead
  of them.** He picked hotspot-nearest and distance-weighted purely by reading the bird
  lists, before any metric could distinguish them. Nearest then scored 9/9 top-9 and
  weighted scored the highest tau of anything tested - neither visible in recall@24, which
  ranked nearest *third*. Recorded explicitly at his request: **judge the lists.**
- **Bar-chart frequency is the answer for accuracy, but cannot be fetched from the app.**
  `ebird.org/barchartData` needs a Cornell SSO cookie (ignores API keys), has no CORS, and
  is ~818KB/region. Viable only as pre-captured static data.
- **No ~200-call method can run live per card.** eBird 429s after ~1,000-2,000 calls; we hit
  it twice in one afternoon from one developer. All users share one embedded key. Caching
  is mandatory, not an optimisation.
- **Hotspot blending is the only genuinely global+instant option** (API key, CORS, no
  pre-loaded data) and is far better than the current method, but structurally short of the
  bar chart: it averages ~200 recent checklists where eBird averages years.
- **The hotspot-recovers-missing-birds hypothesis is rejected.** Three birds (Common
  Ostrich, Speckled Pigeon, African Stonechat) are on Merlin's list but outside Tshwane's
  top 24. James's intuition that the nearby reserve explains the ostrich was reasonable, but
  no hotspot variant surfaced any of the three. Still unexplained.
- **Multi-year historic sampling abandoned**: 516 calls for 16 usable checklists, tau -0.09
  (anti-correlated). Historic single-day hotspot queries are far too sparse; one
  ostrich-heavy Rietvlei checklist put Common Ostrich at #2 on pure noise.
- **No app code changed.** James explicitly declined wiring the Tshwane table in
  ("we wont wire tshwane specifically"). This session is research only.

## What was done

1. Geocoded the test address (Nominatim; full street number failed, street centreline
   worked) to `-25.7834538, 28.2928576`. Saved with the ground-truth list.
2. Confirmed region codes: `ZA-GT` (Gauteng), `ZA-GT-08` (City of Tshwane = Pretoria).
3. Built `probe.py` to score public-API strategies at four radii x three ranking metrics.
   **All scored identically**, which exposed the de-duplication bug - the "different"
   metrics were computing the same thing over a list with no repeats.
4. Established `barchartData` needs SSO (302 to Cornell CAS with an API key). James signed
   in to Chrome; captured both regions via chrome-devtools MCP: 485 + 531 species x 48
   weekly buckets (4/month), saved to `barchart-frequencies.json`.
5. Derived the week-bucket index (`(month-1)*4 + w`, w by day 1-7/8-14/15-21/22+) and scored
   it: **Tshwane 21/24, tau +0.667**, beating province-level on both.
6. Normalised eBird-vs-Merlin naming (Hadeda/Hadada, Dark-capped/Common Bulbul, Cape Turtle
   (Ring-necked) Dove, Cape (Glossy) Starling, Grey/Gray). These are the same birds, not
   misses; initial scoring understated recall by ~4.
7. Built `hotspot-blend.html`, then `compare.html` (5 methods side by side), then
   `budget.html` (3 methods x 5 shrinking API budgets) at James's request - he wanted the
   lists side by side with speeds, to judge himself.
8. Added a **top-9** metric mid-session after his feedback, since recall@24 was not
   capturing what makes a good 3x3 card.
9. Hit HTTP 429 twice. Second time blocked the entire budget sweep.

## Verification

- De-duplication confirmed directly: 183 records / 183 distinct species / max count 1.
- Bar-chart hypothesis scored offline (`score_barchart.py`) and in-browser, agreeing.
- Bar-chart data saved to disk before the SSO session could lapse.
- All five methods in `compare.html` ran clean at least once.
- **Not verified:** `budget.html` has never completed a clean run (rate limited). Its
  numbers are unknown.
- **n=1 throughout.** One location, one date, one hand-typed list.
- **Run-to-run variance is significant** - hotspot-richest moved 17/24 -> 14/24 between
  identical runs. Ignore gaps under ~3 places.
