# Gochujang chicken ramen for four, shopped entirely at Woolworths

Serves 4. Prep 20 minutes, cook 40 minutes.

Built around plain chicken stock (no ramen broth concentrate), boneless chicken breast, and pak choi. If you have thighs, spinach, or the Woolworths broth base instead, each section says how to adjust.

Measurements follow the South African convention: **T is a tablespoon (15 ml), t is a teaspoon (5 ml)**.

## Ingredients

### Broth

- 1.5 L Fresh Free Range Liquid Chicken Stock (3 x 500 ml)
- 4 T Hikari Karu-Mu Koji Miso Paste
- 3 T gochujang paste
- 3 T light soy sauce
- 1.5 T sesame oil
- 4 cloves garlic, finely grated
- 20 g fresh ginger, finely grated
- 1 t sugar

**Building the broth from plain stock.** This recipe uses ordinary liquid chicken stock, not a ramen broth concentrate. Plain stock is thinner and much less salty, so three things change: no water is added at all, the miso goes up to 4 T, and 3 T of light soy sauce goes into the broth itself rather than only into the marinade. Those replace the salt and umami the concentrate would have carried.

If you do have the Asian Chicken Ramen Broth Base 150 ml (R69.99), use the whole bottle, drop to 1 L stock plus 350 ml water, cut the miso to 3 T, and leave the soy out of the broth.

### Chicken

- 4 boneless chicken breasts, about 600 to 700 g
- 4 T light soy sauce
- 2 t sugar
- 1 t salt

**Breast, not thigh.** Breast is lean and has no skin fat to keep it moist, so it goes from juicy to dry in a couple of minutes. Do not air-fry it the way you would a thigh. The two methods below both work, and both are built around not overshooting.

Cook to **74C in the thickest part**, which is the standard safe temperature for poultry. Serious Eats notes that breast meat is at its best closer to 68C and that a full simmer, "just under water's boiling point," overshoots badly, so the aim either way is gentle heat and a thermometer rather than a timer.

### Noodles and toppings

- 2 x 200 g packs Ramen Noodles
- 350 g mushrooms, thick-sliced
- 1.5 T butter
- 1.5 T dark soy sauce
- 400 g pak choi (2 x 200 g Baby Pak Choi, or 1 x 350 g Pak Choi)
- 4 eggs
- 100 g spring onions, finely sliced
- White sesame seeds, toasted

**How much pak choi.** Allow **100 g per person** as bought, so 400 g for four. That is two 200 g packs of Baby Pak Choi, or one 350 g Pak Choi head, which is slightly under and fine. It wilts far less than spinach, roughly halving in volume rather than collapsing to nothing, so do not be tempted to add more.

Baby pak choi: trim the root end and halve each head lengthways, or quarter anything bigger than your palm. Large pak choi: separate the leaves, then cut the white stems into 3 cm pieces and keep the green leafy tops whole and separate. The stems need about 2 minutes longer than the leaves.

## Method

1. **Marinate the chicken.** Mix 2 T light soy sauce with the sugar and salt. Add the breasts and turn to coat. Leave 20 minutes at room temperature, or up to overnight in the fridge. Reserve the other 2 T soy sauce. The salt matters more with breast than thigh, since it helps the lean meat hold onto moisture.
2. **Soft-boil the eggs.** Six and a half minutes in boiling water, then straight into iced water. Peel once cold, halve just before serving.
3. **Bloom the aromatics.** In your largest pot, warm the sesame oil over medium heat. Add the garlic and ginger, fry 60 to 90 seconds until fragrant but not browned. Add the gochujang and the teaspoon of sugar and fry another two minutes, stirring, until it darkens slightly and smells sweet rather than sharp. Do not skip this. Frying the paste is what stops the broth tasting raw and sharp.
4. **Add the stock.** Pour in all 1.5 L of chicken stock and the 3 T light soy sauce. No water. Bring to a bare simmer, not a rolling boil, and hold there for 20 minutes.
5. **Whisk in the miso last.** Ladle a cup of hot broth into a bowl, whisk the 4 T miso into it until completely smooth, then stir that back into the pot. Do not boil it after this. Taste now and expect to adjust, since plain stock starts under-seasoned: more miso or soy for salt and depth, more gochujang for heat.
6. **Cook the chicken, to 74C in the thickest part.** Poaching in the broth is the recommended route: once the broth has had its 20 minutes at step 4, and before the miso goes in at step 5, slip the breasts into the pot and hold it at a bare quiver with no bubbles breaking the surface, 12 to 16 minutes depending on thickness. That keeps the meat tender and pushes chicken flavour into the broth at the same time. To sear instead, pat the breasts dry and cook over medium-high in a little oil, 5 to 6 minutes on the first side without moving them, then 3 to 5 on the second, dropping to medium after the flip if they are thicker than about 2.5 cm. Either way, rest 5 minutes and then cut across the grain into 1 cm slices, since slicing straight out of the pan squeezes the juices out.
7. **The mushrooms.** Slice about 5 mm thick and dry-sear in a wide pan in batches, no oil and no lid, until they have given up their water and browned at the edges. Add the butter, cook a minute, then the dark soy sauce until the liquid has gone and they are glazed. Tip into a bowl.
8. **The pak choi.** Same pan over medium-high heat with a splash of water. Put the halved baby heads in cut-side down, or the white stems first if using a large head, cover, and steam 2 minutes. Add the leafy tops if you separated them, cover again for 1 minute more. The stems should be tender with a little bite left and the leaves just collapsed. Season with a pinch of salt and remove. Unlike spinach it holds its shape, so keep the halves intact and lay them on top of the bowl rather than stirring them through.
9. **The tare.** Wipe the pan, turn the heat to high, add the reserved 2 T light soy sauce, and reduce for one to two minutes to a thick caramel. This goes in the bottom of the bowls.
10. **Noodles, last.** Boil a large pot of water, cook the noodles 3 to 4 minutes, drain, serve immediately. Do not cook them in the broth and do not let them stand in liquid.
11. **Assemble.** Soy caramel into four warmed bowls, then noodles, then hot broth ladled over. Arrange chicken, mushrooms and half an egg on top, and lay two pak choi halves against the side of the bowl. Finish with spring onions, sesame seeds, and a few drops of sesame oil.

Timing: steps 1 and 2 can happen hours ahead. The broth holds on a low flame and only improves, but do not boil it once the miso is in. Chicken and mushrooms hold for 20 minutes. Cook the pak choi and the noodles once everyone is sitting down, since pak choi is best straight out of the pan.

## Adjustments

- **Too spicy.** A teaspoon of honey or sugar into the broth. Gochujang's heat mellows against sweetness, not dairy.
- **Creamy.** A splash of coconut milk or 2 T cream at the end, off the heat.
- **Free depth.** Char an onion half and a few spring onion roots in a dry pan until blackened, simmer in the broth, fish out before serving.
- **Nutty body.** A tablespoon of tahini or smooth peanut butter whisked in with the miso.

Poach the breasts in two batches so the broth temperature does not crash.
